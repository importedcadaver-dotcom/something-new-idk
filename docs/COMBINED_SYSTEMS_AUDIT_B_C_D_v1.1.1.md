## B2. Physical infant capability audit

At six actual months, Acorn and Millie must remain anatomically juvenile brown bears with a plausible weight/size relationship to adult family. Real wild bears are growing mammals with seasonal developmental and maternal dependence; the setting adds extreme cognitive/sapient adaptations. Thus:
- **C:** Acorn reads exceptionally early, plays games, uses toddler-like speech and fears/needs; all are user-selected.
- **C:** Millie may intentionally interrupt her twin and play social games; she is not automatically a strategic adult.
- **OPEN:** Whether he can independently reach power sockets, hold gamepad, work bear keyboard/mouse, access bathroom, climb particular furniture, swim unsupervised, or command voice control from a stationary bed/chair.
- **P:** Design a separate **capability grid** per activity: motor reach, grip, stamina, comprehension, emotional regulation, adult assistance, equipment ergonomics. This guards against an illustrator/simulator making Acorn a dexterous adult human toddler disguised as a cub.

**Failure case:** “Acorn casually types like a grown human on a standard keyboard” would violate his anatomical constraints and unstated motor skill assumptions. **Passing model:** Bear-sized specially adapted paw input, TTS, voice interfaces or staff support—with exact equipment chosen later.

## B3. Two litters, parental biology and family identity

**C:** Two lesbian brown bear mothers; two litters; Rudy older, second litter exactly twins Acorn/Millie; both women are his moms; family is a household/family group. **C:** Cynthia is Audrey's mother. Do not add biological to that relation or assume Rosa and Audrey are legally married. The twin arrangement is supported without a third named parent or a reproductive technology yet.

**OPEN:** Which bear bore each litter, whether the same birth-parent bore both, any donor/assisted reproduction rules, and whether a biological relation to Cynthia is automatically established (it is not). Social “mother” does not imply a chosen gestational route.

**Real-world comparison:** A brown bear has delayed implantation and usually twins; a six-month cub's age is after spring den emergence in northern wild populations, but *this civilization's seasons, birth traditions and physical development are not specified*. Do not impose a wild denning calendar.

**Internal check:** Because Rudy's precise developmental age and actual age remain open, the interval between two litters cannot be fully tested. A plausible interval may exist, but **we cannot certify it without age/gestation decisions**. No inconsistency has been demonstrated.

## B4. Polar girlfriend's family census — specific contradiction risk

**C:** Polar girlfriend's mother had exceptionally prolific multiple litters over decades, resulting in **about thirty biological children/siblings**, with infants through adults, likely most at home, and a household budget stretched despite reasonably paid parents.

**E:** Real wild polar bears usually have small litters, often twins, and lengthy maternal dependence; successful reproduction typically occurs every three years (USFWS). **C:** This fictional mother is unusually fertile; bears here also have much longer lives.

**Paper arithmetic—illustrative, never canon:**
- ~30 children / 10 litters = average 3 each; ten litters could span ~27 years at ~3-year intervals, plus elapsed years depending first/last.
- ~30 / 15 litters = average 2; fifteen litters can span ~42 years at ~3-year intervals, but requires a reproductive life long enough, which is unapproved.
- ~30 / 12 litters = average 2.5; other intervals or mixed litter sizes are possible.

**Risk:** If we later specify standard wild polar-bear reproduction **and** short reproductive lifespan, the family becomes implausible. This is not yet a hard contradiction because the fantasy longevity and exceptional fertility are already approved. **Open:** Whether the girlfriend herself counts among “~30”; first/last litter dates, adult siblings' ages, living-at-home rules, life-stage dependency and parental child-care arrangements.
