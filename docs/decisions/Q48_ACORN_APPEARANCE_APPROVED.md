## 2. User's custom anatomical specifications — approved

These are **not** optional suggestions; the user explicitly added them to Q48:

- **Very chubby / fat / well fed:** Acorn has a notably round, plump, very well-nourished baby-bear body, not simply average cub proportions. His belly/overall baby softness should be evident. This establishes appearance, **not a specific mass, veterinary diagnosis, diet, medical condition or future growth trajectory**.
- **Extra-wide tail:** Acorn's tail is unusually **wide/broad** for his appearance. **Length, exact contour and fur coloration remain unspecified**. Do not replace this with a long fox- or cat-like tail or infer another species.
- **Big, relatively long ears that kind of droop:** Compared with a typical cub of his species, Acorn's ears are **especially big and long enough to droop slightly**. The exact shape, degree of droop, pose-dependent movement, ear color and fold mechanics remain OPEN. Retain naturalistic bear-ear identity, not rabbit or cartoon dog ears.
- **Unusually oversized paws, explicitly larger than Millie's:** Acorn's paws are **abnormally large relative to his own juvenile build**, and **visibly bigger than those of his six-month-old twin sister Millie**. This is stronger than simply “big paws” in selection D. It does **not** establish that he is universally larger than Millie in every dimension or assign her actual measurements.
- **All A–F coexist:** Round face, dense fur, wide tail, oversized ears/paws and chubby body are individual traits layered on a fundamentally realistic, quadrupedal six-month-old brown-bear cub.

## 3. Realism / individual-distinctiveness boundary

The core brief is **naturalistic but unusually proportioned**: distinctly a real brown bear cub, while the user's deliberately exaggerated baby fat, paw size, ear dimensions/droop and broad tail help make Acorn identifiable. These extremes are user-approved fiction and **must not be “corrected” away by assuming every natural bear cub looks identical**.

One genuine design challenge remains for future art: maintain physical readability and a plausible underlying skeletal structure without making the ears rabbit-like, tail extremely long, paws humanlike, or face a stylized anthropomorphic mascot. That is an **art-direction constraint to test**, not permission to reinterpret or reject the user's selected features.
