## Natural-history terminology — researched comparison, not literal construction

**Dam vs den/lodge:** A beaver **dam** is a water-control structure that raises water depth. A **lodge or bank den** is the shelter. Beavers commonly use **submerged entrances** leading through tunnels to dry chambers protected by water and enclosure. This is the closest useful **conceptual inspiration** for Acorn's **small outside gap into a roomier hollowed under-bed area, then a noncramped basement hatch and larger underground refuge**. **Do not add water or submerged access to Acorn's room.**

Research reference: Washington Department of Fish & Wildlife, [Beaver — Lodges and bank dens](https://wdfw.wa.gov/species-habitats/species/castor-canadensis); Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife, [Beavers — Lodges and Bank Dens vs Dams](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/living-with-wildlife/avoid-resolve-conflict/beavers.html); NatureScot, [Understanding the Ecosystem Engineer](https://www.nature.scot/professional-advice/protected-areas-and-species/protected-species/protected-species-z-guide/beaver/understanding-ecosystem-engineer); National Park Service, [Beaver — Lodges vs Dams](https://www.nps.gov/kaww/learn/nature/beavers.htm).
