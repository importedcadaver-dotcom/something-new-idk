## Findings from authoritative wildlife sources

| Question | Supported natural-history answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Does a beaver usually live in its dam?** | **No.** A dam retains water and supports a protective pond environment; a **lodge or bank den** is the actual shelter. | [U.S. National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/kaww/learn/nature/beavers.htm); [Maine IFW](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/living-with-wildlife/avoid-resolve-conflict/beavers.html) |
| **What does the entry lead to?** | Beavers use submerged openings and tunnels leading to protected, dry interior chambers. The submerged access makes it harder for predators to reach them. | [Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife](https://wdfw.wa.gov/species-habitats/species/castor-canadensis); [NatureScot](https://www.nature.scot/professional-advice/protected-areas-and-species/protected-species/protected-species-z-guide/beaver/understanding-ecosystem-engineer) |
| **Are all beaver shelters free-standing lodges?** | No. Some are **bank dens**, excavated into banks; one may be dug beneath stumps/logs/docks. Other beavers build lodges from branches, logs and mud. | [Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife](https://wdfw.wa.gov/species-habitats/species/castor-canadensis); [Maine IFW](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/living-with-wildlife/avoid-resolve-conflict/beavers.html) |
| **Are underwater tunnel entrances always the same number?** | No; authoritative sources describe **one or more** openings (Washington/Maine); exact number varies by structure. | [Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife](https://wdfw.wa.gov/species-habitats/species/castor-canadensis) |
| **Does this make an under-bed hidden basement plausible by itself?** | No. Real-beaver den morphology provides a **spatial analogy**, not evidence of safe excavation, mechanical lifts, hollowed bedframes, egress or noiseless concealment in an estate. | Design inference; not a factual claim about beavers |
