## Research findings — primary sources first

### 1. DougDoug uses customized TTS, rather than generic prebuilt alerts alone

DougDoug's official FAQ states he streams with OBS, has used Streamlabs and StreamElements for subscription alerts, and **now uses his own custom program for donation-message text-to-speech**. It lists voice-control tools used in some broadcasts, but does not disclose complete current custom-TTS features. **Do not treat an unrelated code example as a full account of his private current production software.**

- **Primary source:** [DougDoug Official FAQ](https://www.dougdoug.com/faq), “Software” section (checked 2026-11-08).

### 2. Chat can be assigned an individual synthetic voice and an on-screen persona

His published **ChatGodApp** is a separate public demonstration program that:
- Allows selecting a particular viewer, or letting viewers volunteer into a selection pool;
- Uses synthetic speech to read that selected viewer's messages;
- Supports selectable voice characteristics, including emotion labels such as cheerful, angry and whispering;
- Can optionally drive speaking-related on-screen image movement through OBS integration.

The public code's README names Python, TwitchIO, Azure TTS and optional OBS websocket hooks. The README establishes the model of *chat as a speaking participant*, not that his **current private donation TTS** has exactly the same code/features or price. **We are not installing, executing, cloning or adapting this software.**

- **Primary source:** [DougDougGithub/ChatGodApp README](https://github.com/DougDougGithub/ChatGodApp).
- **Separate tool/prototype distinction:** The specific ChatGodApp sample is not identical to every TTS setup used in DougDoug broadcasts.

### 3. Real-world chat systems require safety controls before a stream's first incident

Twitch's moderation support documentation describes a toolbox with **pre-screening/held messages**, custom channel rules, spam handling, links/terms filters, slow mode, warnings, timeouts, bans and emergency lockdown. Its first-stream guide recommends setting up moderation before going live, protecting personal information on camera and checking preview/audio.

- **Official sources:** [Twitch moderation guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/setting-up-moderation-for-your-twitch-channel?language=en_US) and [first-stream browser guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/stream-from-browser?language=en_US); also [Twitch Creator Camp beginner roadmap](https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1).
- **Caveat:** Twitch is real-world reference material, **not automatically the legal rules or platform identity** of Acorn's fictional bear internet. Fictional setup and rule details remain open.

### 4. Fan sources are illustrative, not authoritative for current fees or policies

A community-run DougDoug wiki describes varied TTS voices, a paid-message system, on-screen messages, joke characters, and moderation interactions. Because it is community-edited and rates/voices may change, **do not assert a precise current paid-TTS threshold or clone a DougDoug joke as canonical Acorn content**.

- **Secondary source, for context only:** [Community wiki, TTS](https://dougdoug.fandom.com/wiki/TTS).
- **Primary evidence priority:** the official FAQ + DougDoug's own published repository + Twitch safety documentation.

## What is actually transferable to Acorn — proposals for later owner approval

1. **Chat has recurring individual voices and personalities**, not just a constant scroll. This supports Acorn's approved Q61 **B speaker-name label**, while the actual first chatters' usernames, species, motivations and relationships are NOT chosen.
2. **Audio and text status are coordinated**: Q61 **A** highlights currently spoken chat; Q60 **G** puts a TTS indicator beside the webcam; Q61 **H** minimizes the indicator when idle.
3. **Pause/skip authority**: Q61 **D** directly empowers Acorn to stop, skip and resume incoming speech; proposed design could also let trusted adult moderators suppress filtered messages, but **specific delegation and controls are still OPEN**.
4. **Pre-screening rather than purely reactive cleanup**: Q61 **E** approves spam/inappropriate-message filtering, with exact automatic and manual rules **OPEN**. A proposed future design should avoid reading flagged material aloud before a moderator can intervene.
5. **Priority is not absolute power**: Q61 **F** permits special treatment for selected approved senders; this is not approval of a permanent speaking queue, viewer payments buying access, or priority over Acorn's refusal.
6. **No visible queue was chosen**: Q61 **C** was not selected. A functioning TTS pipeline could later be described in prose, but do not render a list/queue in the interface without user approval.
7. **Acorn-specific emotional effects must be grounded**: A funny chat reaction or embarrassment needs a previously established trigger/relationship. His baby/toddler-equivalent comprehension, fear, shame, competitiveness, and need for comfort cannot be replaced with adult streamer reasoning.
8. **No unapproved real-world identity**: Don't assume Twitch is the actual platform, that specific DougDoug chat users appear in Acorn's world, or that a DougDoug voice clone/sound effect exists.

## A first-stream design sequence to research — NOT a fictional timeline

Prior to authoring any actual first stream or moderation incident, define or approve:
- **Who Acorn is privately:** comfort needs, attachments, typical emotional regulation and recovery, what counts as embarrassment vs harmless mischief, what motivates him to return to stream.
- **Room and hardware:** camera sightlines, privacy boundaries, a six-month-old quadrupedal cub's reach, giant paws and physically operable control surfaces, who installs/maintains technology.
- **On-camera roles:** his moms' limits/help, who moderates before the first incident, who sees chat, and what permissions Acorn retains.
- **Audience baseline:** why any viewer arrived, size and type of early audience, whether an existing family fanbase was used, first chatters' motivations, how recurring chatters develop without being predetermined.
- **Moderation architecture:** what the filters suppress, who can manually intervene, who can pause/skip or override TTS, what records survive, and how repeated trolls are treated.
- **Chronology:** how his early channel relates to the already-established later viral protest/embarrassment event and previously approved viewer-count ranges.

**Do not write a historical “first stream” right now**. Its dates, titles, messages, first viewer, first moderator, exact gaming skill, incidents and consequences are all not-yet-approved potential future decisions.
