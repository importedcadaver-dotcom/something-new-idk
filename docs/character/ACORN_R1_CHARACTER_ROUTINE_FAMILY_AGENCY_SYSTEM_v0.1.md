## 0. Why this exists

The goal is to specify **a believable baby bear celebrity with independent relationships and real choices**, not a streamer avatar whose family merely reacts to a script, and not a mechanism-focused basement construction game. R1 translates already approved **character dispositions, comfort hierarchy, privacy, and livestream autonomy** into a reusable **text-based causal description** of how a normal life *could* operate, before any historical first stream is designed.

**Three levels of authority, used in every table below:**
- **[APPROVED]** a user-selected or reliably recorded established fact, source-linked.
- **[INTERPRETIVE / PROPOSED]** a plausible way approved facts could interact; optional, *not* adopted canon or a fixed rule.
- **[OPEN]** missing owner choice or physical/technical/safety specification.

**No forced minutes/hours, event frequency, stat points, fixed catchphrases, mandatory lie, dialogue, actions by a cast member, or invented past event.** The “what if?” cases in the QA companion are counterfactual tests only. Nothing advances Acorn's current fictional age or time.

## 2. A flexible ordinary day, NOT a timetable

The original roadmap asks for **ordinary rest/play/gaming/stream/food-care opportunities without assigning clock times or insisting on an hourly routine**. These are **activity contexts** rather than a compulsory linear day.

| Life context | What is approved / known | R1 interpretive design opportunity (NOT user-approved schedule) | Stop / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sleeping / waking** | Sleeping room and private retreats, baby blanket/plush; age-appropriate distress/comfort | He can awaken sleepy, seek private objects, resist getting up or want closeness; family may have their own plans | No specified bedtime, wake hour, sleep requirement, mandatory dream or bed location |
| **Food, care, washing** | Indulgent parents; bath temperature must feel right; toothbrushing often resisted; food gifts can be refused | Ordinary care can interrupt attention and present a real negotiation; parent sees signs Acorn himself misses | No calorie model, medicated intervention, nursing requirement, injury or punitive action invented |
| **Play / exploration** | Toys, tag, swimming, climbing, messy play; sometimes gets stuck when climbing; snakes/centipedes frighten him | Interest can switch from favored toys to a newly noticed stimulus; exhaustion or interference can redirect choice | No forced accidents; supervision and household geography still OPEN |
| **Gaming / learning** | Real Fortnite; initial imperfect game skills, competitive and capable of practice; Mommy skilled and doesn't throw matches | A loss may spark insistence, repeated retries, laughter or requesting help; context makes response different | No fabricated victory rate, controls, ranking, specific VOD or human-hands assumption |
| **Live broadcasting** | Spontaneous type and content; Acorn's ordinary power to start/end and ban/refuse; chat/TTS screen rules | Presence of audience can increase excitement, embarrassment and conflicting social attention; he can independently stop | Staff roles, safeguards, platform identity, exact live schedule and audience history OPEN |
| **Family contact** | Strong different ties to Mama/Mommy/Millie/Rudy/Cynthia; girlfriend part of orbit | Competing interests matter: Mama wants care, Mommy wants game/help/CEO work, Rudy wants autonomy, Millie wants attention, Cynthia wants influence | No universal presence, 24/7 surveillance, invented new sibling dialogue or fixed motivations beyond approved facts |
| **Retreat and emotional regulation** | F+G first principles of context + refusal; objects, then family preferences; H1 and secret H2 | On overstimulation he might ask to be left alone, use favorite plush, seek Mama or reject help before reconsidering | Not every retreat is H2, not every upset mandates gaming, not an automatic hiding-time clock |
| **Transition between contexts** | Cub has physically distinct suite rooms and independent channel | Finishing a game can be derailed by tiredness, Mama checking on him, Millie requesting an object, or his own spontaneous changed mind | No invented room/door geometry or teleportation, and no forced story outcome |

**Proposed open-ended routine composition:** A day may *offer* several of these contexts in varying order and length. Character intention + physical opportunity + attention/fatigue + family actions determine which actually takes place. Do **not** encode an exact 9:00–10:00 breakfast/school/stream schedule before approval.

## 4. Proposed behavioral causality chain (not a fixed algorithm)

For any ordinary situation, reason in this order:

1. **Observable opportunity:** Where is the character? What can their bear body actually sense/do? What rooms and objects can they access? Avoid narratorial omniscience.
2. **Current personal intention:** What does this character want to continue or protect? It must not simply be “help Acorn” for every family member.
3. **Development and available energy/attention:** Acorn is young, plump and easily overwhelmed in some contexts; he reads well but does not reason like an adult. Other family members have separate obligations.
4. **Relationships and remembered experiences:** Millie teasing, Rudy admiration, Mommy genuine gaming competence, Mama protection, Cynthia fear, the **already known** viral embarrassment. Do not fabricate new memory flags.
5. **Boundary or competing claim:** Acorn's stream/room choice, a sibling asking entry, Mama's caregiving responsibility, staff technical role, etc. Some disputes cannot be solved by a baby yelling “I'm CEO.”
6. **Possible response set, not predetermined script:** Acorn may refuse, ask, hide, negotiate, self-soothe, seek Mama, become upset, switch activities, or accept help. A particular response needs relevant context and must remain **optional**.
7. **Observable short-term consequence:** A person might back off, stay concerned, be disappointed, lose time, keep an intention or seek another trusted relative. Consequences should reflect actions rather than random punishments. **No exact outcome canonized**.
8. **Future relational implication:** A minor incident *could* change trust, mood or willingness next time; avoid converting one disagreement into permanent hatred, obedience or a named past event.

This is a **writer/systems reasoning checklist**, not a formula, stat model, probability table, scheduled agent loop or gameplay authorization.

### Proposed emotional micro-patterns, each conditional

- **Disappointed in Fortnite:** Frustration or retry may precede embarrassment; or he may accept Mommy's help, laugh, switch game or end stream. The existence of Mommy's skill and refusal to intentionally lose is APPROVED; a named fight over one match is not.
- **A desired toy is unavailable:** Acorn can search, demand, accuse or comfort himself; Millie's *general* toy interference is established, but she must not be proven culprit for **every** missing item.
- **Needs care while broadcasting:** He may resist interruption, then soften toward Mama or insist on finishing his turn. Parent emergency boundaries are not yet chosen.
- **Afraid of Cynthia:** He may become quiet, avoid eye contact or seek more trusted relatives; Grandma does not become omniscient or a default source of comfort.
- **Shamed by mention of known viral incident:** Existing embarrassment is canon; reaction may be mild, upset, banning a user, refusing to discuss or seeking solitude. No new historical chatter named.
- **Hiding under bed:** His known H2 and small *outer gap* exist, but which location he uses is neither universally observable nor automatic. Mama does **not** magically know H2 because she sees his bed.

## 5. Decision authority and child-care boundary — PROPOSED LAYERS, rules NOT yet ratified

**Already user-approved:** Acorn holds ordinary stream and studio control; Q66 grants Mama/Mommy necessary care/emergency access; staff facilitate; no established automatic absolute child sovereignty during danger.

| Decision class | Approved default | **PROPOSED** way to reason about an unresolved conflict | Remains OPEN; do not imply approved powers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary show content/guest/moderation** | Acorn directs whether to go live/end, what to play, guests, bans, refusing donations | Staff may explain consequences or technical limits and execute his direction; caregiver may ask rather than clandestinely usurp | Formal staff/mod command permissions, accidental misclick safeguards |
| **Ordinary room privacy** | People ask; sleeping room private, playroom relatively welcoming, ordinary studio guests Acorn-approved; siblings ask even for toys | Mama/Mommy might request entry for care, Acorn may ask for a moment, and both can have emotions | Exact knocking etiquette, domestic routines, staff room access |
| **Routine care disagreement** | Parents have caregiving authority, love and involve themselves; Acorn resists brushing/baths at times | Distinguish a harmless preference (timing, toy, game) from necessary care; offer choices rather than equate any refusal with emergency | Which care actions are nondeferrable and who decides |
| **Clear and serious safety emergency** | Q66 caregiver/emergency rights persist | Proposed principle: a responsible adult **can intervene to protect him**, but intervention scope should be proportionate and not rewrite his ordinary channel ownership | Actual severity threshold, proof, permissions, response chain, platform/bear law; **not canon yet** |
| **Tech or stream outage** | Bear tech staff support; Acorn's ordinary authority remains | A technical failure may limit what can happen without transferring moral or channel ownership | Who can physically disconnect, recover account, pause mic/TTS, loss of service |
| **Private H2 unknown to parents** | Parents explicitly ignorant, even as caregivers | Parents may notice ordinary clues or respond to observable danger; they cannot respond to basement location without evidence | Discovery policy, emergency rescue/egress and how child-safe construction is possible |

**Critical:** Do not silently let Mama “shut down the stream whenever she dislikes a game” or let Acorn forbid all care forever. Neither absolute is directly selected. In later R2, obtain owner approval of the **high-stakes policy boundary**, not a new permission table for every toy/door.

## 8. Lifecycle of a *potential* social interaction — conceptual, not enacted

```text
Available activity / intention
  -> Character-specific observation and knowledge
  -> Body + toddler attention + situational emotion
  -> Different characters choose their own goals
  -> Boundary/consent/care question arises?
       -> no: continue/redirect activity
       -> yes: conflicting requests, refusal, negotiation, care concern
  -> One of multiple plausible responses
  -> Observable short-term result (without forced victory for Acorn)
  -> Possible future trust/memory implication (not automatic)
```

The process must accommodate **non-events** (a parent passes by without noticing, chat is quiet, siblings are elsewhere) and **failed intentions** (a character tries to approach but is refused) without inventing dramatic spectacle.

## 9. Remaining R1 choices — do NOT turn into micro-interviews

**High impact / OWNER CHOICE required before finalizing rules:**
1. The **ordinary care vs urgent safety intervention boundary**, including streaming, staff and parental authority. No new platform rules may be asserted until R2.
2. Whether daily life has any **default rhythm or spontaneous freeform approach**; no fixed minute-by-minute timetable is approved.
3. How far to take **emergent character behavior in a future medium**: this is an unimplemented proposal, not evidence of an already-authorized NPC simulator.

**Lower impact / acceptable to defer:** Plush identity, bedtime exact minute, particular cabinet contents, number of daily snacks, H2 lever force, precise sibling dispute frequency, narration format, first named chat member.

**R1 working conclusion:** The approved cast and emotional canon are rich enough to make **multiple plausible reactions** intelligible. The remaining need is a consolidated governance choice and an R2 physical streaming system, **not another basement hatch option**.
