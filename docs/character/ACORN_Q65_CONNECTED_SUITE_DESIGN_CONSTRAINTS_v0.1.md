## 2. Spatial relationship and safety design — possible constraints to evaluate, not new canon

- **Direct internal connectivity:** The approved word *connected* means Acorn has personal spaces linked for ordinary movement; the exact number or positions of doors, internal corridor, step-free vs graded access, open archways and transitions are **not yet specified**.
- **Cub-accessibility:** Consider naturalistic quadrupedal six-month brown-bear posture, very fat body, enlarged paws and limited reach when planning routes and inputs. Do not add anthropomorphic hands to make an ordinary human desktop work.
- **Hiding nook from Q64:** A secret/personal retreat exists. It could be in sleeping or play space, but neither placement is approved yet. Whether cameras cover it or family members know it remains undecided.
- **Family intervention:** Since he can initially refuse comfort (Q63 G) and can get physically overwhelmed (Q62 D), privacy must be reconciled with age-appropriate caregiver safety. Exact rights, access permissions, doors/locks and emergency overrides require user approval.
- **Streaming boundaries:** No assumed 24/7 cameras through the sleeping room, recorded family arguments, unrestricted stranger access to the home or compulsory sharing of his den details. Specific privacy policy still open.
- **Free movement:** A family cub may carry a toy/blanket between rooms or prefer to nap somewhere unusual. Identifiable room functions do not mean perfect compliance or that the cub becomes tethered to a single room.
