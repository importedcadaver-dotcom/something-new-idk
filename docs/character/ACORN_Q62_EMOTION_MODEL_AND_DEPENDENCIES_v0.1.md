## The seven approved capacities and their relationships

Rather than treating Acorn as a creature with one permanent “mood,” Q62 approves **context-dependent emotional behavior**:
- **A frustration** can lead to protesting, trying again or refusing help.
- **B embarrassment** can lead to withdrawing/face-covering and later memory of being seen.
- **C fear/distress** can lead to seeking reassuring closeness from an appropriate trusted family member.
- **D overload** can reduce patience, clarity of baby speech and cooperation when he is tired, overstimulated or pressured.
- **E guilt** is possible after hurting/upsetting someone, but admitting responsibility and making amends may be difficult.
- **F toys, routines, pleasant activity** can help him regulate without magically canceling outcomes or relationships.
- **G context/relationships** changes these behaviors with Mama, Mommy, Millie, Rudy, Grandma Cynthia or unfamiliar bears.

These can overlap or occur at different times. An embarrassed Acorn might **also** want comfort, but this is not an approved fixed sequence. A bratty, intensely competitive gamer can also be sensitive and affectionate. Neither a single bad interaction nor wealth automatically fixes or ruins his relationships.

## Interpretive design principles (proposals; no numeric model approved)

| Topic | Useful consistency test | Must NOT assume |
|---|---|---|
| Emotion cause | Link visible reaction to frustration, fatigue, embarrassment, attachment or relationship | Random tantrum without a trigger; always-chaotic baby |
| Intensity | Behavior can vary by context and trust | Fixed crying chance, hard-coded response thresholds or a simulation engine |
| Body | His very fat six-month cub body and huge paws matter for huddling, withdrawal or comfort-seeking | Human hand gestures, bipedal toddler posing |
| Speech | Under stress he may be less clear, per Q62 D, yet his default voice is high-pitched and babyish | Adult sentence structure or total loss of language on every upset |
| Memory | Embarrassment may be remembered; consequences of real mistakes persist | Permanent specific trauma, exact recall duration, or instant memory deletion |
| Comfort | Familiar toys/routines help; trusted family can reassure | Specific beloved toy, identical caregiver response, mandatory nursing |
| Morality | Guilt/apology can be sincere yet developmentally difficult | A cynical adult apology script or a rigid universal punishment system |
| Social differentiation | The same cue can mean different things depending on Mama, Mommy, Millie, Rudy, Cynthia, stranger | One-size-fits-all reaction to every person |

## What needs definition before a first-stream history

1. **Relationship-specific emotional anchors (Q63 proposed):** who Acorn normally approaches, resists or avoids when upset; preserve both mothers' caring roles and already established Cynthia fear. Don't invent a family event.
2. **Concrete private interests and comfort environment:** decide actual toys, habits, favorite routines and which experiences make him feel secure; no default object or routine yet.
3. **Physical room:** where a quadrupedal, plump cub naps, stores toys, can hide, or approaches a camera; each room/design choice requires owner consent.
4. **Computer/setup:** actual bear-operable game inputs and assistance boundaries; keep his channel authority and TTS pause/skip from Q61 without implying he personally programs it.
5. **Relationships and background:** who encouraged his gaming/streams, consistent family behavior and chronology relative to the known later viral incident.
6. **Then** community genesis and early moderation/stream chronology: prevent arbitrary first-viewer names, fake numbers, invented incidents and forced actions.

All six bullets are **design planning**, not extra lore.
