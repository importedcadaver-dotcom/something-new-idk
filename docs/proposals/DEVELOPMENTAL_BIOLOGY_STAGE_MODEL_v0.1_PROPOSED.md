## Purpose

Design physically coherent but fictional biological development for Acorn, Millie, Rudy, the two mothers, Grandma Cynthia, and Rudy's unusually populous polar-bear in-law household, while preserving the user's explicit decisions:

**Approved:** 1 actual bear year ≈ 4 human years of *developmental/cognitive* age **during development**; age 6 actual months ≈ human developmental age 2 years; bears live 80–120 **actual years**; anatomy remains mostly realistic/quadrupedal with enhanced paw dexterity; Acorn's exceptional reading and gameplay ability are genuine exceptions; Millie and Acorn are same-age twins; Rudy a teenage-equivalent high schooler; Cynthia elderly; fantasy polar bear mother has ~30 offspring from exceptionally prolific litters. The long-lifespan world is speculative; do not transplant wild bear death ages.

**Not approved:** precise physical growth curve, age of cognitive maturation, reproductive onset, nursing/weaning, fertility duration, school entry, legal majority, adult/elder classification, age/status of Rudy, Audrey, Rosa, Cynthia, the girlfriend, or the girlfriend's parents.

## Source ledger — external baseline, not fictional canon

1. San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance / Brown Bear: born hairless/eyes sealed, commonly two cubs, eyes open at about six weeks, maturity 3–5 years; wild and managed lifespans much shorter than fantasy. https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/brown-bear
2. San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance / Brown Bear Fact Sheet: birth 400–500 g, first-year cubs, nursing ~1.5–2.5 years, adult sexual maturity around 3.5–5.5 years with differences by sex. https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/brownbear/reproduction
3. National Park Service / West Cook Inlet Bear Ecology: brown bear sexual maturity ~4–6 years, twins common and lactation ~1.5–2.5 years. https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/coastal-bear-ecology.htm
4. Animal Diversity Web / Brown Bear: brown bears may continue growing physically to 10–11 years even though sexual maturity is around 4–6 years; 6-month mass estimates vary. https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Ursus_arctos/
5. San Diego Zoo / Polar Bear: polar bear maternal dependence to ~2.5 years, newborns very small and helpless. https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/polarbear/reproduction
6. U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service / Polar Bear conservation materials: small litters, intervals of years, later reproductive maturity. https://www.fws.gov/species/polar-bear-ursus-maritimus

These sources establish only facts about *real wild/managed bears*, not the speculative HoneyDweller world. Any fictional exception must be explicit and traceable to owner choices.

## 1. Six separate developmental axes

Do **not** use one generic "bear age" to force unrelated properties.

| Axis | What it describes | Approved currently | Still to decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Calendar age | Actual elapsed bear years, always 12 actual months per year unless calendar is separately defined | Acorn/Millie 6 months; CEO succession 17 *actual* years ago | Fictional calendar seasons, year duration if different |
| B. Cognition & emotional development | Speech, theory of mind, memory, self-control, literacy, reasoning | Approx. 4 human developmental years per actual bear year *during development*; Acorn exceptional reading | Developmental-stage cutoffs, whether growth plateaus in young adulthood |
| C. Body size and shape | Mass, height, proportions, fur, teeth, claws, joints, stamina and motor reach | Juvenile naturalistic quadruped anatomy, enhanced paw dexterity | Whether real bear physical growth timeline applies or shifts; species differences |
| D. Reproductive maturity | Puberty, fertility, gestation, breastfeeding and nursing/weaning | Two litters, fantasy high-fertility polar mother, extended overall lifespan | Fertility and nursing ages and lifespan of fertility |
| E. Social/legal status | School, adulthood, parental obligations, employment, marriage, alcohol law, livestreaming permissions | Rudy in high school; Acorn has ordinary channel authority; family-group residence custom | Legal ages, developmental appropriateness; do not infer from puberty |
| F. Senescence/longevity | Adult stable years, aging, declining strength, cognitive health, elder age | 80–120 actual-year species lifespan; Cynthia elderly | When aging begins, individual variance, whether species differ |

### Modeling principle (PROPOSAL)
Let **C(t)** be mental/developmental milestones, **P(t, species)** physical maturity, **R(t, species, individual)** reproductive state, **S(t, society)** legal/social status, and **L(t)** aging/senescence. These functions have shared calendar input but must **not be forced identical**.

## 3. Candidate integrated stage scaffold — PROPOSED ONLY

These physical descriptions are **illustrative** based partly on real bear developmental timing. They are **not** finalized as universal rules, milestones, educational ages or reproductive rights.

| Actual age | 4× cognitive age if appropriate | Candidate *physical* description under real-anchored model | Possible social label (NOT selected) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 0–6 weeks | ~0–0.5 yr | Tiny, vulnerable, eyes initially closed; nursing/neonatal thermoregulation | Newborn |
| 6 weeks–3 months | ~0.5–1 yr | Eyes open; increasing coordination, moving and exploring | Early cub |
| 3–6 months | ~1–2 yr | Small mobile cub; improving play and locomotion; very juvenile body | Little cub |
| **6–12 months** | **~2–4 yr** | Visibly baby/cub-sized relative to adults; climbing, running and growing motor skills | Older baby / little cub |
| 1–2 years | ~4–8 yr | Yearling; much bigger, still developing strength and senses; potential maternal care | Juvenile |
| 2–3 years | ~8–12 yr | Growing juvenile; not necessarily fully sized or independent | Older juvenile |
| 3–4 years | ~12–16 yr | Subadult body, teenage cognitive stage, increasing capability | Adolescent |
| 4–5 years | ~16–20 yr | Near adult in some physical traits; puberty may be possible depending species | Older adolescent / young adult |
| 5–6 years | ~20–24 yr | Social/mental mature young adult if selected; still possible physical growth | Young adult |
| 6–10+ years | Mature | Reproductive/physical completion varies, some growth can continue | Adult / physically maturing |
| 10+ years | Adult experience continues | Prime long-lived adult; timing of senescence to be decided | Adult, later elder ages OPEN |

**Design risk:** With 80–120-year lifespan, mature adulthood is much longer than youth. Is this desirable? No automatic slowing of juvenile cognitive milestones should be assumed solely to keep a human-proportion childhood.

### Stage naming conventions (PROPOSAL)
“Cub” can be a **physical descriptor** (small, immature, dependent) and a **social descriptor** (child). They may diverge. Someone can be mentally school-age while still physically a juvenile bear, or physically near adult before reaching legal social majority. Formal legal majority must not be inferred directly from sexual maturity.

## 4. Four candidate physical-growth architectures for Q44

**A. Wild-anchored early growth (recommended default for realism):**
- Physical newborn/cub/yearling/subadult stages follow actual bear timing broadly: infancy visibly natural, reproductive capacity around ~4–6 actual years, physical size continues up to ~8–11 or later by species.
- Juvenile cognitive development obeys the selected 4× equivalence separately.
- **80–120-year lifespan is achieved by unusually prolonged adult viability / slower senescence**, not by stretching cubhood to decades.
- Requires an explicit reproductive-senescence model later; long lifetime does not automatically mean fertility for 100 years.

**B. Mildly stretched juvenile growth:**
- Preserve a clear baby at six months and natural bear shape, but have prolonged juvenile physical stages and potentially later adult size, e.g. full growth 10–15 years (**example only**).
- Reproductive/mental/legal ages stay independently adjustable.
- Risk: physically youthful bodies with mentally adult characters for longer; need comfort/body ownership rules.

**C. Accelerated early physical growth:**
- Bear cub remains little at six months but reaches physically adult size more closely to early cognitive adulthood (~5–7 actual years).
- Could simplify certain teenage body/education comparisons; is a stronger biological fantasy exception versus real bear growth.
- Risk: loses some prolonged naturalistic young-bear stages.

**D. Independent axes/species and individual differences:**
- Baseline physical timing chosen separately for brown, polar, other bears; each individual can vary with genetics, health, nutrition.
- Cognitive 4× stays established; adults may have diverse growth and skill trajectories.
- It is a **modifier system**, not enough alone to decide whether base physical maturation is A/B/C.

**E. Hybrid user-customizable:**
- A baseline broadly anchored to real early bear growth and physical cub cuteness; separately tune puberty, nursing, adult size, and later aging using species-aware rules.
- Balance against approved long-life and family structure without adopting a one-size-fits-all human developmental/body model.
- Requires explicit decisions for defaults and overrides, not blanket permission to retcon biology.

## 5. Nursing, reproduction and independence are distinct decisions

**Real reference:** Brown bears may nurse into their second year or beyond while gradually eating solids; wild cubs may stay with mothers 2–3+ years. In the HoneyDweller world, that would overlap with a **human-equivalent developmental age around 6–10** at 1.5–2.5 actual years. This is an unusual human comparison, but the animals remain bears and their developmental curves are decoupled.

**Open choices:** Maintain wild-like physical nursing despite fast cognition? Evolve accelerated weaning in sapient bears? Vary by individual and species? Recognize social comfort nursing independently of full milk dependence? No one choice is approved.

Likewise, long adult life and fantasy high-fertility polar bears do not answer:
- First reproductive age; gestation/delayed implantation status; postpartum recovery;
- fertility vs physical/social/legal adulthood;
- litter-size distribution, sibling age spacing and household economies;
- later reproductive senescence vs whole 80–120-year lifetime.

Do **not** copy nursing choices from other bear game projects.
