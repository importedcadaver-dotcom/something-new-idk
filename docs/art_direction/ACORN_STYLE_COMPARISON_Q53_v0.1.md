## 0. What is being compared — and what is fixed

The owner approved researching and comparing all candidates **A, B, C, D, E, F**, rather than choosing a final art style. The goal is to find out which presentation most strongly conveys Acorn as an individual while retaining game/stream readability. **A–E** propose distinct character rendering or world-rendering approaches; **F** is an on-screen streamer **framing/UI context** that can use any character art style A–E. It is therefore **not a mutually exclusive alternative** and should be tested as a separate layer.

All six briefs below refer to **the same already-approved character**. None adds fictional eye color, birth age, markings, or a new personality trait:

- **Acorn “Muffin” HoneyDweller**, male, **six actual months old**, recognizable **brown bear**, principally quadrupedal with real bear limb/face structure.
- Round baby head, fat cheeks, extremely **chubby/fat/well-fed** torso, extra-thick fluffy coat, short stout legs.
- Anatomically bear-like but **abnormally big paws**, larger than twin Millie's; a **lighter fur sock on his own anatomical front-left paw**.
- **Very big, relatively long, somewhat drooping bear ears**, **one** ear colored deep nearly-red cinnamon. **LEFT/RIGHT EAR HAS NOT BEEN SELECTED**.
- **Unusually broad tail**, with some cinnamon fur, but **not** a long fox/cat tail.
- Hazel eyes whose exact geometry is unknown, with emotional expressiveness that does not turn them into human/cartoon eyes.
- **Dark brown body and back**, **almost entirely white belly**, cinnamon mixed in as **fine guard hairs**, warm cinnamon cheek/muzzle accents and some tail fur. No unapproved spots/side stripes/shoulder badges; the fur sock is **not automatically white**.
- Existing streaming personality: high-pitched baby voice is normal, expressive toddler syntax and game vocabulary, sweet/bratty/competitive/chaotic/sensitive/curious; eight content types with no fixed template. Visual style must not make him an adult bear, animal mascot or generic human toddler.

**Source of truth:** [approved Q53 choice](../decisions/Q53_BROAD_STYLE_COMPARISON_APPROVED.md), [visual canon sheet v0.2](../ACORN_VISUAL_CANON_SHEET_v0.2.md), individual Q48–52 decision entries. Any contradictions in future drafts are failures to be corrected, not new lore.

## 1. Parallel comparison scenarios (proposals, no depicted scenes)

For a future *authorized* comparison, prepare analogous frames/views, not random unrelated images. These are **test compositions**, not story scenes or claims they already occurred.

1. **Full-body 3/4 standing reference, neutral background.** Same character scale in each treatment, paws (especially front-left sock), broad tail silhouette and the white belly visible where anatomically possible. Chubby silhouette must remain recognizable.
2. **Face close-up with neutral/happy/annoyed/tearful expression studies.** Preserve hazel irises, warm cheek/muzzle accents, realistic muzzle and large somewhat drooping ears. These are expression-design *labels*, not in-world events.
3. **Side and back anatomical orthographic views.** Confirm body depth, fat belly, broad tail and cinnamon guard hairs. Verify the coat is not replaced by arbitrary decorative patches.
4. **Streamer-camera framing study.** Acorn viewed at realistic relative scale from within a cub-usable gaming space, with nearby bear-adapted inputs; this is a composition *brief*, not an authorization to pick hardware or render an image now.
5. **Overlay/readability study.** A hypothetical gaming viewport, webcam area, scrolling chat, TTS/donation indicators and Acorn's controls competing for attention; no channel handle, font, logo, sponsor or chat names are canon.

**Orientation rule:** A future camera may invert left/right in a preview, but Acorn's **anatomical** front-left sock must stay on the same limb and be labeled relative to him. For the ear, **do not depict a definitively left or right red ear as settled canon**; resolve that decision first, or test clearly labeled *temporary* left/right hypotheses with no approval implied.

**Unknown-color rule:** In text only, call the sock **lighter** and his one red ear **deep reddish cinnamon**. Don't assign fixed HTML hex codes. Sample palettes would be later artist hypotheses, not adopted canon.

## 2. Candidate A — Photorealistic wildlife

### Visual intent
A convincingly living six-month-old brown bear cub photographed with natural lens/fur/lighting physics and clearly real bear paws, ears, muzzle and stature. The unusual body shape, broad tail and large drooping ears are presented as individual bear variations rather than costume accessories.

### Character treatment
- High-fidelity layered fur: dark brown guard coat scattered with warm cinnamon strands; deep rich red-cinnamon on the single ear; near-white belly is actual fur, not a painted garment.
- Fat cheeks, round muzzle and thick soft chest/belly; an anatomically believable broad tail; enlarged forepaws with proper digits/claws.
- Expressions through ears, eyes, head tilt, posture and muzzle muscles; no human eyebrows or plastic smile.
- Hazel irises visible in head close-ups; in wide shots expect them to be subtle.

### Presentation/stream applicability
- Effective for webcam-like content and nature/household cutaway images.
- A real video-game interface or fantasy bear technology must be visually integrated to avoid feeling like a wildlife photo pasted onto a Twitch screen.
- Luxurious household/studio lighting has to retain credible animal scale and surface contact.

### Art risks and QA
- **Major risk:** Making every slightly unusual feature look physically impossible or accidentally selecting a normal bear tail and losing the owner-approved broad tail.
- Hazels and fine front-left sock likely subtle in distant compositions.
- Photoreal realism could make extreme cuteness/emotional expressivity harder to read during gameplay-like thumbnails.
- Avoid a generic indistinguishable stock bear photo and artificial over-sharpening.

### Hypothesized strengths
Highest species/fur fidelity; strong convincing fur. More challenging for consistent stylized expression and "streamer persona" readability.

## 3. Candidate B — Premium stylized 3D game

### Visual intent
A collectible-level premium realtime/CG game character—expressive and memorable, with preserved bear skeletal/paw/muzzle anatomy and sophisticated fur. Compared with A, features and expressions are intentionally *art-directed*, but never changed into humanoid limbs or plush construction.

### Character treatment
- Deliberately accentuate user-approved huge paws, chubby silhouette, broad tail and oversized drooping ears.
- Fur rendered with natural directional flow, dark brown primary tones and discreet cinnamon guard hairs; big fluffy cheek volume without false painted eyeliner.
- Rigging/art-direction idea: ears, cheeks, body sway, paw positions and head gesture form an expression vocabulary. This is a prospective animation design test, not motion implemented.
- Hazel eyes remain bear-structured even when improved for face-camera reading.

### Presentation/stream applicability
- High flexibility for camera distance, facial expression, changing set design and future 360° viewpoints.
- Strong candidate to put inside a live webcam region at consistent perspective.
- Needs a bear-operable controller/interface design; no human-finger animation.

### Art risks and QA
- **Major risk:** Defaults to glossy, generic animated-film mascot style, a “small human in bear fur,” or smooth hairless vinyl when the user wants realistic cub fluff.
- Easy to over-enlarge eyes instead of communicating through anatomically plausible brow/ear movement.
- Asset consistency across sister Millie and recurring bear guests would require distinct models, not recolored clones.
- Higher hypothetical character/rig/lighting production burden than some 2D approaches.

### Hypothesized strengths
Strongest prospective expressive three-dimensional character staging; significant quality-control and implementation requirements.

## 4. Candidate C — Detailed hand-painted 2D

### Visual intent
Rich authored illustration with naturalistic brown-bear outlines, dense brush-textured fur and warm visual atmosphere, while keeping forms consistent. This is **not** a generic watercolor teddy bear or a Disney-styled simplification.

### Character treatment
- Dark-brown coat with deliberate cinnamon colored guard-hair strokes, rich deep red-cinnamon ear, warm cheek/muzzle fur and carefully layered white belly tones that still read **white**.
- Exceptionally round cheek/belly silhouettes, realistic bear muzzle, slightly drooping enlarged ears, heavy forepaws.
- Use painted texture/color separation to keep the front-left sock visible even at smaller display size; do not silently turn it into a stark white sock.
- Naturalistic highlights make hazel eyes readable in face close-ups without altering iris anatomy.

### Presentation/stream applicability
- Attractive for close-up character portraits, overlays, stickers and narrative cutaway panels.
- Requires strict model sheets for front, 3/4, side/rear and emotion variants to avoid inconsistent paw placement, tail shape, or eye layout.
- Perspective of background, camera, game capture and 2D character must be deliberate so it does not feel pasted on.

### Art risks and QA
- **Major risk:** Different images arbitrarily reposition the red ear or paw sock or change the length of the broad tail.
- Brushwork may overpower fine character-identifying features.
- Excessively cute flat eyes and a plush outline can erase bear anatomy.
- If layered UI is busy, painting complexity needs visual simplification at thumbnails.

### Hypothesized strengths
Warmth, deliberate personality and attractive stills; maintaining continuity across many poses is the key difficulty.

## 5. Candidate D — Modern detailed pixel art

### Visual intent
A carefully pixel-authored 2D character with enough sprite resolution to show six-month brown-bear mass, oversized paws, slightly drooping big ears, broad tail and readable sock. Prioritize coherent pixel scale and clear silhouettes, not tiny generic blobs.

### Character treatment
- Dark main coat plus limited warm-cinnamon ramp; the broad white belly should remain clearly distinguishable from highlights and sock.
- Scattered fine cinnamon guard hairs may need *few strategically placed warm pixels* rather than literal hair-by-hair rendering, so it remains legible without introducing spots or stripes.
- Hazel irises likely require a face portrait or high-resolution sprite variant; don't claim both large detailed irises and tiny pixel eyes can be legible simultaneously.
- Anatomical left-front sock is an important side-specific feature; **do not mirror a base sprite without fixing the markings**.
- Avoid exaggerated human arms/hands as a shortcut to interactive expression.

### Presentation/stream applicability
- Works well for game-like overlays and pixel environments when UI uses matching pixel density and scale.
- Thumbnail webcam presence depends on sprite size and whether the cub portrait is used alongside full-body sprites.
- Not every modern streaming capture must itself use pixel art; a mismatched UI/camera style requires intentional integration.

### Art risks and QA
- **Major risk:** The very wide tail, front-left sock, red ear, cinnamon cheek hairs and hazel eyes cannot all be readable in one tiny sprite.
- Overdetailed single-pixel noise creates “AI slop” or visual mud; a hand-controlled sprite palette/silhouette check is required.
- Mirroring errors can swap side-specific markings.
- A tiny sprite can accidentally make Acorn thin and long-legged.

### Hypothesized strengths
Strong iconic character silhouettes and low-resolution scene readability if output sizes are chosen appropriately; requires deliberate scale-specific assets.

## 6. Candidate E — HD-2D / 2.5D

### Visual intent
A character and environment presented with dimensional depth, layered scenery and a cinematic camera while retaining detailed 2D illustration/pixel art or billboarded elements. Style can mix naturalistic fur painting with dimensional background if consistently lit.

### Character treatment
- Acorn appears small and very plump in a believable den/studio; large paws and white belly visually distinct as camera changes.
- Layered fur and ear/tail silhouettes must be coherent under rotation or swapped view-specific sprites.
- Shadows and contact points must match bear quadrupedal stance.
- Fine cinnamon guard hair and iris readability depend on resolution and camera distance.

### Presentation/stream applicability
- Strong potential for a game-view scene that includes Acorn, his plush surroundings and interactive equipment.
- More challenging for casual real-world webcam framing unless there are intentional camera transitions or a separate portrait view.
- A 2.5D environment with a flat billboard character can look pasted together; lighting, ground contact, perspective and occlusion must match.

### Art risks and QA
- **Major risk:** Incorrect perspective and camera-facing sprite orientation make a fat bear appear thin, or move the ear/sock from side to side.
- Layered shadows may turn the all-white belly cream or hide the identifying paw.
- Hybrid assets from wildly different render styles lose visual unity.

### Hypothesized strengths
Rich immersive environments and premium visual staging; consistent character and camera perspective are difficult.

## 7. Candidate F — Realistic streamer UI (presentation layer)

### Visual intent
An actual believable broadcast frame: game footage/reaction material + a bear-sized webcam region with Acorn + readable chat/moderation/donation indicators + streaming overlay. The user asked for Acorn as a **real streamer personality**, so the interface needs the same credibility as the cub.

**This is a framing/design test, not its own required rendering medium.** It can be paired with A/B/C/D/E.

### Character treatment
- Camera/framing must show a six-month baby bear using plausible bear-adapted gaming equipment with visible cub physical scale; do not float a bear head over a human-size keyboard without explanation.
- When the webcam is tiny, all approved colors/traits still exist but some (hazel eyes, fine guard hairs) may not be visually distinguishable; a larger character/portrait view may be needed elsewhere.
- A 3/4 camera view or secondary angle can help read the broad tail and sock, **but camera choice is OPEN**.
- Side-specific sock must remain on anatomical left even if a hypothetical screen uses mirrored self-view.

### Interface hierarchy (candidate design, not canon)
1. **Core content**: Fortnite or another genuine game, reactions, Just Chatting or outdoor camera. Never make an imaginary game substitute for real Fortnite.
2. **Acorn visible**: a webcam/cub camera that gives clear reactions and feels proportionate to a young bear.
3. **Chat legibility**: responsive audience but not every message huge, avoiding overcrowding.
4. **TTS, moderation, donations**: present when relevant, not permanent intrusive decorations; no automatic forced food delivery and no mandatory eating.
5. **Optional branding**: user-selected channel handle/fan den/overlay color not chosen yet; do not invent names or sponsor advertisements.
6. **Family participation**: may appear if an actual planned stream would involve them, but do not force every relative into every on-camera frame.

### UI-specific risks/QA
- **Major risk:** Crowded overlays obscure Acorn, the real game and the chat content simultaneously.
- AI-generated on-screen fake gibberish text, random badges or impossible screenshot layouts would reduce credibility.
- Streamer framing can be realistic even if the cub is illustrated; don't conflate video realism with character photorealism.
- Avoid mixing an unrelated, highly stylized family UI with an otherwise premium stream layout.
- Respect the unresolved fictional platform choice; do not assume literal Twitch terms, logo or branding.

### Hypothesized strengths
Direct fit for the approved stream premise and potential showcasing of Acorn's actual personality; **should be combined with at least one primary rendering style** rather than scored as if it were a standalone illustration style.

## 9. Comparison rubric and acceptance gate (proposal)

A future reviewer can mark each criterion **PASS / CONCERN / FAIL**, then choose **top priorities** before scoring:

1. **Identity fidelity (critical)** — Dark-brown dominant body/back; nearly white belly; cinnamon guard hairs/face/tail; red ear on one side yet unselected side until decision; front-left light sock; hazel eyes; no extra patches.
2. **Body fidelity (critical)** — Distinct six-month naturalistic bear, exceptional chubbiness, huge paws, broad tail, large soft-drooping ears, quadrupedal stance.
3. **Face/cuteness** — Rounded cheeks, baby proportions, nuanced expressions without humanized eyes or Disney-like mascot simplification.
4. **View consistency** — Same individual from full body, 3/4, rear, close-up and webcam, especially asymmetric markings.
5. **Streamer credibility** — Bear-usable equipment, believable room scale, appropriate game captures, readable TTS/chat and **no forced sponsorships or invented channel handle**.
6. **UI consistency** — Overlay fonts/colors/readability intentionally match world aesthetic; no crowded generic web dashboard.
7. **Character comparison scalability** — Future Millie and other family members each remain distinguishable; do not make them clone/recolors of Acorn.
8. **Asset/production practicality** — How much work to maintain multiple expressions, angles, environments and apparel without losing identity.

### Non-negotiable FAIL conditions for any eventual authorized image

- Two red-cinnamon ears (only one approved); **guessing** left/right as the canonical side without user choice.
- Sock mirrored to anatomical **front right**, or silently adding matching socks.
- White belly replaced by tan/beige, or dark brown body replaced by predominantly orange/golden fur.
- Giant paws shrunk to ordinary paws, tail made long/cat-like, ears made rabbit/dog-like, or fur becomes plastic/toy fabric.
- Acorn turned humanoid, adult bear, or generic flat-eyed cub; species and baby proportions lost.
- Real Fortnite represented as a simplistic fictional toddler game when actual-game gameplay is a focal point.
- Twitch/YouTube logos or specific brand sponsorships presumed without story-world authorization.
- Creator references used to copy their characters, catchphrases, jokes, or copyrighted overlay layouts.
- Placeholder sample art subsequently recorded as approved canon without user signoff.
